Enterprise AVL

Technology Designed Around the Way Business Works

Explore Solutions → How We Compare →

Enterprise AVL specializes in professional audio, video and collaboration environments for businesses and commercial organizations.

A modern workplace demands more than screens, cameras and speakers. Conference rooms have to connect instantly. Presentations need to work without friction. Remote participants need to feel present. Training environments need to engage. Technology should make collaboration easier—not become another obstacle employees have to overcome.

Enterprise AVL combines commercial audio, video, and lighting (AVL) designers, industry-trained technical professionals and specialized installation teams who understand today's workplace and the technologies businesses depend on.

Technology should disappear into the meeting—not become the meeting.

From Installation to Adoption.

We don't consider a conference room successful because every component passed a technical test.

We consider it successful when your people walk into the room and know how to use it.

Our team guides customers through consultation, design, installation, programming and commissioning—and then trains the people who will actually use the environment. The result is technology your organization can confidently put to work.

    The Running Tuba Process

    How We Compare

    Use this to evaluate any provider—including us. The goal is a fair comparison of total capability and value, not the lowest number on a page.

    Evaluation Area Our Standard Questions to Ask Any Provider
    Industry-Specific Discovery Discovery is led by specialists who work in corporate workplaces every day and start with how the space is actually used. Who will lead discovery, and how many projects like mine have they personally worked on?
    Relevant Design Expertise Systems are designed around the workflows, sightlines, acoustics and operators specific to corporate workplaces. Will the design be tailored to my environment, or adapted from a standard room template?
    Specialized Installation Experience Installation teams are experienced in corporate workplaces and the access, scheduling and safety realities that come with them. Who is physically performing the installation, and are they employees or subcontracted labor?
    Scope Completeness Proposals identify infrastructure, programming, integration and training up front—not as later change orders. What is explicitly excluded from this quote, and what would trigger a change order?
    Commissioning & Testing Every system is tuned, tested and validated against the design intent before handover. What does your commissioning process include, and will I receive the test results?
    User Training Training is built into the project for the people who will operate the room—not a rushed handoff on the last day. How much training is included, who delivers it, and what happens when staff turn over?
    Documentation As-built drawings, configuration files, credentials and operating guides are delivered as part of closeout. What documentation do I receive at closeout, and do I own the configuration files?
    Manufacturer Training & Certifications Technical staff maintain current manufacturer training on the platforms we design and deploy. Which manufacturers is your team currently trained on for the products you are proposing?
    Service & Support Support is structured around response expectations, escalation paths and a team that already knows your system. What are your response times, and who answers when something fails during a live event?
    Lifecycle Value Design decisions account for serviceability, upgrade paths and the useful life of the platform. How long will this platform be supported, and what does the upgrade path look like in five years?

    This comparison describes how Running Tuba approaches projects and offers neutral questions worth asking any provider. It makes no claims about any specific competitor.
