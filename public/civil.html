Civil AVL

Technology Designed for Public Service

Explore Solutions → How We Compare →

Civil AVL specializes in professional audio, video, and lighting (AVL) systems—including the communication and presentation technology—for government agencies and public-sector environments.

Public technology has a different standard. Council chambers, courtrooms, public meeting spaces, emergency operations centers and government facilities require systems designed around reliability, clarity, accessibility, security and long-term operation.

Civil AVL brings together public-sector-focused system designers, industry-trained technical professionals and specialized installation teams who understand the requirements and responsibilities surrounding government technology environments.

When technology supports public service, reliability isn't optional.

Designed for the People Who Depend on It.

We guide stakeholders through system planning, design, installation, programming, testing and commissioning—and we don't stop there.

Civil AVL trains administrators, operators and end users on the systems they'll depend on, helping ensure technology is understood, confidently operated and capable of supporting the public experience it was designed to serve.

The Running Tuba Process

How We Compare

Use this to evaluate any provider—including us. The goal is a fair comparison of total capability and value, not the lowest number on a page.

Evaluation Area Our Standard Questions to Ask Any Provider
Industry-Specific Discovery Discovery is led by specialists who work in public-sector facilities every day and start with how the space is actually used. Who will lead discovery, and how many projects like mine have they personally worked on?
Relevant Design Expertise Systems are designed around the workflows, sightlines, acoustics and operators specific to public-sector facilities. Will the design be tailored to my environment, or adapted from a standard room template?
Specialized Installation Experience Installation teams are experienced in public-sector facilities and the access, scheduling and safety realities that come with them. Who is physically performing the installation, and are they employees or subcontracted labor?
Scope Completeness Proposals identify infrastructure, programming, integration and training up front—not as later change orders. What is explicitly excluded from this quote, and what would trigger a change order?
Commissioning & Testing Every system is tuned, tested and validated against the design intent before handover. What does your commissioning process include, and will I receive the test results?
User Training Training is built into the project for the people who will operate the room—not a rushed handoff on the last day. How much training is included, who delivers it, and what happens when staff turn over?
Documentation As-built drawings, configuration files, credentials and operating guides are delivered as part of closeout. What documentation do I receive at closeout, and do I own the configuration files?
Manufacturer Training & Certifications Technical staff maintain current manufacturer training on the platforms we design and deploy. Which manufacturers is your team currently trained on for the products you are proposing?
Service & Support Support is structured around response expectations, escalation paths and a team that already knows your system. What are your response times, and who answers when something fails during a live event?
Lifecycle Value Design decisions account for serviceability, upgrade paths and the useful life of the platform. How long will this platform be supported, and what does the upgrade path look like in five years?

This comparison describes how Running Tuba approaches projects and offers neutral questions worth asking any provider. It makes no claims about any specific competitor.
